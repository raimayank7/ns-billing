GST invoice format: what a tax invoice must show

A tax invoice is the bill a GST-registered seller gives when charging GST. The buyer uses it to claim input tax credit, so a missing detail can cost your customer money and you a reminder from your accountant. Here is the list from Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, in plain words.

The checklist

DetailWhat it means
Your name, address and GSTINExactly as registered.
Invoice numberA running number, up to 16 characters, unique in the financial year. Letters, numbers, hyphen (-) and slash (/) are allowed. See invoice number rules.
Date of issueThe day you make the invoice.
Buyer's name, address and GSTINGSTIN if the buyer is registered.
Buyer details for big B2C billsFor an unregistered buyer, when the bill is ₹50,000 or more: name, address, delivery address, and the state's name and code.
HSN code (goods) or SAC (services)4 or 6 digits depending on your turnover. See HSN and SAC codes.
Description, quantity and unitWhat you sold, how much, and in what unit (Nos, Kg, Ltr…).
Total value and taxable valueThe value before tax, after any discount.
Rate and amount of taxCGST and SGST/UTGST, or IGST, shown separately, plus cess if any.
Place of supplyThe state where the supply goes, with its name, for a sale to another state.
Delivery addressIf different from the buyer's address.
Reverse chargeWhether tax is payable by the buyer under reverse charge (Yes/No).
SignatureYour signature or digital signature, or that of someone you authorise. (Not needed on an e-invoice.)

The invoice maker has a field for every one of these, and the preview shows them in this order.

How many copies

  • Goods: three copies. Original for recipient, Duplicate for transporter, Triplicate for supplier.
  • Services: two copies. Original for recipient and Duplicate for supplier.

In the invoice maker, choose the label under Notes and look › Copy label and print once for each copy.

When to issue it

  • Goods: before or at the time the goods are removed or delivered.
  • Services: within 30 days of providing the service (45 days for banks, insurance companies and some financial businesses).

Small bills to unregistered buyers

When a single sale to an unregistered buyer is under ₹200 and the buyer does not ask for an invoice, the rules allow one consolidated invoice for all such sales of the day instead of a bill each time.

Common mistakes

  • Charging CGST + SGST on a sale to another state (it should be IGST). Which tax to charge.
  • Restarting or repeating numbers in the same financial year.
  • Leaving out the buyer's GSTIN, so the buyer can't claim credit.
  • Writing "GST 18%" as one line without splitting it into CGST 9% and SGST 9%.
  • Issuing a tax invoice when you are a composition dealer (you should issue a bill of supply).
This page explains the general rules in simple words. Rules change through notifications, and special cases exist (exports, SEZ, e-invoicing, specific industries). Check with your accountant or the official CBIC and GST portal pages before relying on it.
