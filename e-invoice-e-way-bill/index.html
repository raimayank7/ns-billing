E-invoice and e-way bill: who needs them

Two government systems sit next to your invoice. An e-invoice is your invoice registered on a government portal. An e-way bill is a permit for moving goods. Neither is made by an ordinary invoice maker, including this one.

E-invoice (IRN)

  • Who: businesses whose aggregate turnover crossed ₹5 crore in any financial year since 2017-18, for B2B invoices, exports and some notes. Certain sectors are exempt (for example banks, insurance companies and SEZ units).
  • How: the invoice details are sent to the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP). It returns an IRN (a reference number) and a signed QR code, which must appear on the invoice.
  • Time limit: businesses with turnover of ₹10 crore or more must report an invoice to the IRP within 30 days of its date.

If e-invoicing applies to you, the invoice you make here is not a valid e-invoice until it is reported on the IRP. Use the government portal or software connected to it.

E-way bill

  • When: goods worth more than ₹50,000 in one consignment are moved. Some states set a different limit for movement inside the state.
  • Who makes it: the supplier, the buyer or the transporter, on the e-way bill portal, before the goods move.
  • What it needs: the invoice number and date, GSTINs, HSN codes, value, and vehicle or transporter details.
  • Some goods don't need one, for example many unprocessed foods, and movement over very short distances in certain cases.

What you can do with this invoice maker

  • Make the invoice itself, correctly numbered, with HSN codes and the right tax. Those details are what the IRP and the e-way bill portal ask for.
  • Keep the transporter's copy: choose Duplicate for transporter as the copy label.
  • Small businesses below the e-invoice limit can use these invoices as they are.
Limits and exemptions change through notifications. Check the current position on the GST, IRP and e-way bill portals or with your accountant.
