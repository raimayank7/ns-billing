Bill of supply: format and when to use it

A bill of supply is a bill without GST. It is issued when the seller can't charge tax: either the seller is in the composition scheme, or what is sold is exempt from GST.

Who issues a bill of supply

  • Composition dealers: they pay tax at a fixed rate on turnover and are not allowed to collect GST from buyers.
  • Registered sellers selling exempt goods or services, for example fresh fruit and vegetables, fresh milk, or certain education and health services.

When one sale has both taxable and exempt items, a single invoice-cum-bill of supply may be issued.

What it must show

  • Your name, address and GSTIN.
  • A serial number, up to 16 characters, unique in the financial year.
  • Date of issue.
  • Buyer's name, address and GSTIN if registered.
  • HSN or SAC code, description and value of the goods or services.
  • Your signature or that of someone you authorise.

Composition dealers must also write: "Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies". The bill of supply maker prints this line for you.

As with tax invoices, a bill is not needed for a sale under ₹200 to someone who doesn't ask for one, as long as a daily summary is kept.
